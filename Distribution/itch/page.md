## Description

**The perfect simulation of an audio cassette for your Mac – just like in 1986.**

*"Fucking skeuomorphism, and not even good one."*  
— a Reddit user

Guilty as charged. It's the simulation of a cheap tape recorder, not a high-end audiophile component. A cassette on your Mac desktop. Tape whatever your Mac is playing – a song on Spotify, a clip on YouTube, an Instagram reel (if you REALLY have to) – or your own voice introducing the next song. Flip it over, scribble on the label, and send it to a friend. They don't even need the app: the tape plays in any browser.

Recording is fiddly, just like back then in 1986. You can only record by actually listening to the music while it plays – no dragging songs into a playlist. It takes time, it's a pain in the …, but it's actually fun. And you make the effort, because it's for that one special friend.

### The story behind it

I'm old enough to have done this for real. In 1986, where I grew up – in the south of Germany near the Czech border (it was the Czechoslovak border back then, just to be exact) – our local record store only had country music. Swapping tapes with friends at school (and after) was the only way to hear anything new. We put real effort into every mixtape, because we made them for each other. To be honest, I wouldn't have known about The Cramps or The Vaselines without them. A good tape was a sign of friendship.

Tapes stayed with me. In Berlin in the 90s, cassettes were still the medium of the underground – demos, mixtapes and live recordings, passed from hand to hand.

That's the feeling Mixtape App brings back – into the digital age. You're not recording for yourself. You're recording for your friends.

**Make the perfect mixtape. Share it with friends.**

### What it does

- **Just a cassette.** No window, no Dock icon – only the tape floats on your desktop. Hover over it and the deck appears.
- **Records what your Mac plays** – Spotify, YouTube, anything – or the microphone.
- **Like real tape:** recording overwrites what was there, each side holds half the running time (C46, C60, C90), and you flip the cassette for side B.
- **Four tape types:** Ferro, Chrome, Metal and Clear.
- **Hand-written label and drawings** right on the cassette.
- **VU meters and a REC LEVEL fader** that saturates like analog tape when you push it into the red.
- **Tape sound** – hiss, wow & flutter, spool noise, key clunks, pause squeal – each can be switched off.
- **Send it:** a tape is a single .mixtape file. Attach it to an email; your friend double-clicks it and the cassette appears.

### Install

1. Download Mixtape.dmg, open it and drag Mixtape onto Applications.
2. Mixtape isn't signed by Apple yet, so macOS blocks it the first time. Double-click Mixtape, click **Done**, then open **System Settings → Privacy & Security**, scroll down and click **Open Anyway**. Only needed once.
3. The first time you record, allow Mixtape to record system audio or the microphone.

The guide "Read me first" inside the disk image explains each step.

Requires macOS 14.4 or later, Apple silicon or Intel.
